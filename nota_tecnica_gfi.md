## Abstract

We present the **GeoFuego Index (GFI)**, an operational seasonal fire-risk indicator published as a three-level scale — **Seasonal Fire Risk: LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH** — for every Argentine jurisdiction, updated daily. The index combines two measured terms. An **ENSO component** sets the base level: 25 fire seasons of satellite active-fire detections (2001–2025, MODIS and VIIRS, intra-sensor anomalies) conditioned on the Oceanic Niño Index at a three-month lead, calibrated separately for four regions, **each measured over its own fire-season window** — a signal that is direct and robust in the northwest (+50% under La Niña, *p* = 0.005), direct and suggestive in the northeast, **inverse and suggestive in Patagonia** (El Niño above La Niña at every lag tested in its December–March season, consistent with the documented fuel-buildup mechanism), and **not measurable in the semi-arid centre**, where the indicator declares the absence instead of emitting a level. A **zone-level drought term** then escalates the level: the country's fire activity is decomposed into 116 *fire zones* (weighted k-means over a 0.25° density grid of 2.39 million VIIRS detections, 2012–2024); each zone carries a daily Drought Code (DC) series from ERA5 reanalysis, and when the 30-day mean DC of **any** zone in a province exceeds that zone's frozen monthly threshold, the province steps up one level. The escalation rule is deliberately non-averaging: a danger notice is not averaged, and the provincial mean measurably dilutes local extremes (Paraná Delta, August 2020: triggering zone at its 91.7th percentile while the provincial mean sat at the 83.3rd). A climatology-based season gate keeps out-of-season jurisdictions at LOW while still letting drought escalate as an early signal, and a country-extent rule raises the national level when drought escalation reaches two-thirds of the provinces. In hindcast over 2012–2024 the drought term fires in 15.4% of zone-months, and **72.3%** of those record above-median fire activity in that same zone against a **53.6%** base rate — a precision, not a probability of detection, which is **20.8%**: the term is deliberately conservative, accurate when it speaks and silent through most events. Its Peirce skill score is **0.116** (95% CI 0.077–0.153, year-block bootstrap) against **0.002** for a pure monthly-climatology predictor matched on firing rate, so the drought term carries information the fire calendar alone does not; it also adds information daily fire-weather indices do not carry, since half of its activations fall in months whose monthly Fire Weather Index is not extreme. Skill decays with lead time (72.3% at lag 0 to 59.0% at three months), and the five-month anticipation of the 2020 Paraná Delta crisis is a case study, not a measured lead-time skill. Leave-one-year-out re-derivation leaves these figures essentially unchanged: the rank-based threshold rule is out-of-sample by construction on its trigger side, the per-year skill is positive in each of the 13 years, and the declared ENSO signs survive all 25 folds (significantly out of sample only in the northwest, *p* = 0.006); the prospective record, registered publicly since deployment (2026-09-27), remains the validation on data the calibration never saw in any form. The GFI is a probabilistic seasonal risk factor; short-term danger quantification remains the role of daily fire-weather indices, and alerting authority rests with the official agencies. The framework itself is territory-agnostic — density-derived fire zones, a regionally calibrated ENSO term and per-zone drought thresholds transfer to any territory with a sufficient satellite fire record and a documented ENSO teleconnection; Argentina is its first operational deployment.

## 1. Introduction

ENSO modulates South American precipitation and, through fuel accumulation and curing, regional fire-season severity. Operational systems use this knowledge indirectly — seasonal outlooks incorporate ENSO through expert prose (e.g., NIFC, AFAC, Argentina's SNMF) or implicitly through coupled forecast systems feeding fire-weather indices (EFFIS/SEAS5). Reviewing the publicly documented operational products we could access (September 2026), **we are not aware of a fire-danger product in which ENSO phase enters as an explicit, calibrated term**; this was not a systematic survey and we state it as the limit of our knowledge rather than as an established absence. The direct scientific antecedent, Chen et al. (2011), forecast South American fire-season severity from sea-surface temperatures at continental scale but did not transition to operations; a key obstacle is that the ENSO–fire relationship changes sign and strength across regions. The GFI addresses this non-stationarity by regional calibration — each region measured over its own fire-season window, with the sign changing across regions exactly as the literature predicts — and by declaring, region by region, where the relationship is not measurable at all rather than imposing one: of four regions, three carry a usable signal (two direct, one inverse) and one does not (§3.1). Its drought term adds what an ocean-state predictor alone cannot see: the drought actually accumulated on the ground, measured where the fires historically occur.

The framework is territory-agnostic: decomposing a national active-fire record into density-derived fire zones, calibrating an ENSO term per region and a drought threshold per zone applies to any territory with a sufficient satellite fire record and a documented ENSO teleconnection (as in Amazonia, Indonesia or Australia). Argentina is its first operational deployment.

Argentina's fire phenology motivates both terms. In the subtropical north the fire season is out of phase with temperature: the wet, hot summer grows fine fuel, the dry winter cures it, and peak burning occurs in late winter–early spring (16 of 24 jurisdictions concentrate ≥ 56% of detections in May–October, including the eight most fire-active provinces), while Patagonia is the mirror — winter precipitation, summer drought, January–February peaks. What sets the fire season is the timing of the dry season, not of the heat; the previous wet season's rainfall governs how much fuel exists to cure, which is the physical pathway for a lagged ocean-state predictor. The same phenology makes the drought term seasonal by construction: the Drought Code integrates the drying that converts standing fuel into available fuel.

**Relation to official products.** Argentina's official daily fire-danger map (SMN/SNMF) is computed from the national surface-station network with a five-class scale and a 72-hour horizon; at the seasonal scale the SNMF publishes a fixed high-season calendar per regional (both accessed 2026-09-26). The GFI complements both: relative to the daily map it adds the seasonal axis; relative to the fixed calendar it adds a data-derived monthly climatology per jurisdiction, ENSO-phase modulation, and a drought term measured inside each province — a season outlook that shifts with the observed state of the Pacific and of the ground instead of repeating every year.

## 2. Data

- **Active fires:** NASA FIRMS. VIIRS S-NPP 375 m for 2012–2025 (fire zones, climatologies, drought validation; filters: confidence ≠ low, `type=0`); MODIS Terra/Aqua (confidence ≥ 30, `type=0`) extends the ENSO calibration back to 2001. Series are never mixed across sensors: ENSO anomalies are computed **intra-sensor** (each month normalized by the median of that calendar month from the same sensor), which removes the cross-sensor detection-capacity factor (measured at ×3.1–3.5 nationally, non-scalar across regions). The zone-building archive holds **2,394,573 filtered VIIRS detections** (2012–2024), each assigned to its province by point-in-polygon against official IGN boundaries.
- **ENSO:** Oceanic Niño Index (ONI), NOAA/CPC — three-month running mean of SST anomaly in Niño 3.4, official thresholds (El Niño ≥ +0.5 °C, La Niña ≤ −0.5 °C), each trimester assigned to its central month.
- **Meteorology for the drought term:** ERA5 reanalysis sampled at each fire-zone centroid via the Open-Meteo archive API — noon local values plus 24-hour accumulated precipitation, 2011-10-01 to present (the first 92 days are Drought Code spin-up and are discarded).
- **Regions and climatologies:** NEA, NOA, Centro, Patagonia (province-to-region mapping declared in the calibration file); provincial monthly climatologies are VIIRS 2012–2025 medians. The in-progress 2026 season is excluded from all calibration.

## 3. Method

The public scale is **Seasonal Fire Risk** with three levels — **LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH** — per jurisdiction and for the country. The level is built in four declared steps: an ENSO component sets the base, a zone-level drought term escalates it, a season gate bounds it, and a country-extent rule aggregates it. All thresholds are fixed by convention and declared before evaluation; none is tuned to the hindcast.

### 3.1 ENSO component (base level)

The ENSO term conditions each region's fire-season anomaly record on ENSO phase. For each month, the anomaly is the detection count divided by the median of that calendar month (same sensor); the seasonal factor for a phase at lag *L* is the median of anomalies **within the region's own fire-season window** over all months whose ENSO phase *L* months earlier was that phase. The window is derived from the data, not chosen: it is the set of calendar months carrying at least 8% of the region's annual detections (VIIRS 2012–2025) — the same threshold the season gate uses (§3.3) — which yields August–November for NOA (81% of its fire), July–October for NEA (64%) and the country (61%), December–March for Patagonia (65%), and a bimodal January + August–October + December for Centro (71%). An earlier version of this note applied a single July–November window to all four regions; that window measures the northern season and captured only ~25% of Patagonia's fire, so Patagonia's teleconnection was not being measured at all (§6.1b). Months whose climatological median is below 5 detections are excluded (unstable ratio). Significance is assessed by permutation test on log-anomalies (20,000 iterations), reported for both contrasts — La Niña vs. rest, which tests a direct signal, and El Niño vs. rest, which tests an inverse one; all *p*-values are nominal and uncorrected (§6).

**Calibrated factors (lag 3, own-window seasonal-anomaly median by phase, 2001–2025).** *n* is the number of month-observations of the region's window in each phase, not independent seasons:

| Region (window) | La Niña | Neutral | El Niño | *n* (Niña / neutral / Niño) | *p* (Niña vs. rest) | *p* (Niño vs. rest) | Signal used |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| NOA (Aug–Nov) | **1.50** | 1.25 | 0.92 | 12 / 73 / 15 | **0.005** | 0.255 | direct, robust |
| NEA (Jul–Oct) | 1.20 | 1.02 | 1.02 | 12 / 74 / 14 | 0.198 | 0.428 | direct, suggestive |
| Country (Jul–Oct) | 1.26 | 1.07 | 1.03 | 12 / 74 / 14 | 0.217 | 0.549 | direct, suggestive |
| Centro (Jan + Aug–Oct + Dec) | 1.10 | 1.10 | 0.86 | 22 / 77 / 26 | 0.547 | 0.266 | **none** (never significant at any usable lag) |
| Patagonia (Dec–Mar) | 0.87 | 0.88 | **1.16** | 34 / 32 / 34 | 0.306 | 0.119 | **inverse, suggestive** |

**The operational lag defines the signal.** The engine reads the lag-3 factor, so the qualitative signal type is declared only where the *sign* holds at lag 3 **and** is stable across the lag sweep {0, 3, 6, 9, 12} (Appendix A). Under that rule the four regional verdicts are: **NOA** direct and robust — the sign holds with nominal significance at lags 0, 3 and 6 (*p* = 0.005, 0.005, 0.047). **Patagonia**, measured in its own season for the first time, is **inverse at all five lags** — El Niño sits above La Niña in every column of the sweep, with the largest separation at long lags (lag 9: 1.39 vs. 0.98, El Niño vs. rest *p* = 0.009 nominal). The direction and the lag structure are both consistent with the documented mechanism for northern Patagonia (Kitzberger & Veblen 1997; Kitzberger, Swetnam & Veblen 2001): El Niño's wet springs build the fine-fuel load that cures and burns in following dry seasons. The signal is declared **suggestive, not robust** — it does not reach nominal significance at the operative lag (*p* = 0.119) and no cell survives correction for the 50 tests of the sweep. **Centro**, whose sign under the old northern window flipped four times across lags, stabilizes to direct at lags 0–9 under its own window but never approaches significance (*p* ≥ 0.27 at every lag the engine could use); it remains declared **none**. NEA and the country hold a direct sign at lags 0–6 with weak support. Lag 3 remains the operative lag for engine and publication alike; the lag-0 and lag-3 subsamples differ (30 vs. 12 La Niña month-observations in NOA's window), so nominal-*p* comparisons across lags partially reflect a change of sample.

Daily, the engine reads the latest published ONI trimester, determines the phase, and assigns each province a base level through declared rules on the regional lag-3 factor *f*. When the phase matches the region's declared signal (La Niña for a direct signal, El Niño for an inverse one), the base is *elevated* if *f* ≥ 1.10, and *critical* if in addition *f* ≥ 1.45, the signal is robust, and the upcoming window is climatologically heavy (*r* ≥ 1, §3.3); when the phase opposes the declared signal, the base is *low* if *f* ≤ 0.90; in every other case it is *normal*. The three cutoffs are conventions fixed before evaluation, not tuned to the hindcast. The internal four-level classification maps onto the public scale as low → LOW, normal → MEDIUM, elevated and critical → HIGH. Where no ENSO signal is measurable — Centro — the indicator declares the absence ("local climatology governs") instead of emitting an uninformative level: covering a region means stating what the data support there, including nothing. Only the robust NOA signal can reach the critical tier; Patagonia's suggestive inverse signal can raise the base level to elevated during El Niño phases, and the season gate (§3.3) keeps that from surfacing outside its December–March season as anything but a forward-looking note.

### 3.2 Fire zones and the drought term

**Fire zones.** Fire activity within a province is strongly clustered, and a drought measurement is only meaningful where the fuel is. Each province is decomposed into up to five *fire zones*: the 2012–2024 VIIRS archive is binned onto a 0.25° density grid (the ERA5 resolution) per province, and a deterministic count-weighted k-means (greedy farthest-point initialization, then Lloyd iterations; no random seed) places k = min(5, occupied cells) centroids where the historical fire mass is. The result is **116 zones** covering all 24 jurisdictions, each carrying the fraction of its province's historical detections and a cardinal descriptor ("northwestern", "central", …) used in the product's plain-language statements. Sanity checks are anatomical: Entre Ríos' leading zone (33% of its detections) is the Paraná Delta opposite Rosario; Córdoba's is the Sierras; Santa Fe's mass sits in the Bajos Submeridionales.

**Drought term.** Each zone carries a daily **Drought Code** (DC, Van Wagner 1987) series computed from ERA5 at its centroid, 2012 to present. The DC is the slowest component of the Canadian Fire Weather Index system — a deep-layer moisture deficit with a time constant of weeks, which makes it the natural seasonal-scale term. The zone's operational drought state is the **30-day trailing mean of daily DC**, compared (to two decimals) against a frozen threshold for the **calendar month in which the window ends**.

Two properties of that threshold are declared because both matter for reading every figure below. First, **the climatology is built from the operated statistic itself**: for each zone and calendar month, the 13 values are the 30-day trailing means evaluated on the last day of that month over 2012–2024 (window [end−29, end], minimum 25 days of data, leave-nothing-out), and the stored threshold is the 11th-smallest of the 13. What production compares against the threshold is the same quantity the threshold was derived from — an earlier calibration derived it from calendar-month means instead, and §6.5b documents that mismatch and its correction. The residual approximation is one of granularity: thresholds are monthly, so a trailing window ending mid-month is compared against the end-of-month climatology of that month. Second, with *n* = 13 the rule fires for the top one or two values of the record: the **measured exceedance rate is 15.4%** of zone-months (2,789 of 18,096), not 10%. We keep the label p90 for continuity with the calibration file, but the operative definition is *top-2 of 13*. Thresholds are frozen in the versioned calibration file (calibration 2.2.0; 116 zones × 12 months).

**Escalation rule (A-zonal).** If the current 30-day DC of **any** fire zone of a province exceeds its monthly p90 threshold, the province's level steps up **one** level, capped at HIGH. There is no provincial averaging and no weighting: **a danger notice is not averaged**. The provincial weighted mean measurably dilutes local extremes — in August 2020 Entre Ríos' mean DC sat at its 83.3rd percentile, below threshold, while its Delta zone stood at its **91.7th percentile** over 7,973 detections (median August: 78). The product names the triggering zone in the level's one-line statement.

### 3.3 Season gate

An above-median ENSO factor in a month when a jurisdiction essentially never burns is not actionable, so the level is gated by the jurisdiction's own fire calendar. A prior, degenerate case is resolved before any gating: a jurisdiction with no relevant historical fire activity (total monthly climatology below 24 detections; the City of Buenos Aires is the one case) reports LOW with an explicit statement that the indicator does not apply there. Over the province's monthly detection climatology, the engine weighs the upcoming four-month window with decreasing weights (4, 3, 2, 1) — the current month counts most, the horizon least — and normalizes against a uniform year (ratio *r*). A jurisdiction is **out of season** when *r* < 1.0 **and** the current month carries less than 8% of its annual fire activity; out-of-season jurisdictions report LOW with an explicit forward-looking statement. The two conditions are jointly necessary: the share test keeps the northern season's October tail lit when the decreasing window already looks at summer, and the window test keeps flat-calendar Patagonian provinces dark in their winter. The drought term escalates **in any gate state** — accumulated drought outside the season is an early signal, not noise.

Because the share test is a *necessary* condition, the gate can never switch off a month carrying ≥ 8% of a jurisdiction's annual fire: that property is algebraic, guaranteed by the rule's construction, and is therefore not evidence of the gate's calibration (§4).

### 3.4 Country-extent rule

The national level is the country's ENSO base level plus one step (capped at HIGH) when zone-level drought escalation is **extended**: at least two-thirds of the jurisdictions (≥ 16 of 24) drought-escalated in the same month. The product's statement declares the count ("N of 24 provinces with fire zones above their historical threshold DC").

The two-thirds threshold is a **convention chosen a posteriori** over a sweep of candidate counts, and this is stated plainly because the sweep's own output shows why the choice cannot double as evidence: the fraction of active months with above-median country fire rises with the count (83.3% at ≥ 14, 96.3% at ≥ 15, 100% from ≥ 16 up), so reporting the value at the selected count measures the knob, not the rule. Two-thirds was retained because it is a conventional extent criterion with a pre-existing meaning, not because it maximised anything.

## 4. Hindcast and validation

All figures below are measured on the 2012–2024 archive with the production engine and frozen thresholds; no threshold was adjusted to improve them, with the one declared exception of the country-extent count (§3.4). The hindcast is published in the product (calendar "× GFI" view, 2012–present). Every figure in this section is reproduced by the scripts in the deposit (§7).

**Zone-level skill.** Assigning each of 2.39 million detections to its fire zone gives 18,096 zone-months (116 zones × 156 months). Contingency table, with the event defined as above-median fire activity in that zone for that calendar month:

| DC below threshold | 7,684 | 7,623 | 15,307 |
| **total** | **9,701** | 8,395 | 18,096 |

|---|---|

| False alarm ratio (FAR), *b*/(*a*+*b*) | **27.7%** |

| **Peirce skill score (POD − POFD)** | **0.116** (95% CI 0.077–0.153) |
| Heidke skill score | 0.110 |

**The 72.3% is a precision, not a hit rate**, and we name it so explicitly because *hit rate* conventionally denotes POD in the fire-danger and verification literature, where it would be 20.8%. Read together, the two numbers describe a deliberately conservative predictor: it fires in 15.4% of zone-months against an event frequency of 54% (bias 0.287), so it stays silent through four of five events, and when it does fire it is right 72.3% of the time against a 53.6% baseline. For an escalation rule whose cost of a false alarm is a jurisdiction published one level too high for a month, that asymmetry is the intended design, not an artefact — but it must be reported in both directions.

Confidence intervals are computed by **year-block bootstrap** (resampling the 13 years, not individual zone-months): the 30-day trailing mean and the shared regional weather make adjacent zone-months strongly autocorrelated, and a naive binomial interval on the precision would read ±1.7 pp where the block interval reads **65.2–78.2%**.

**Skill against a non-trivial baseline.** Against chance the scores above are positive but modest; the operative question is whether the drought term beats the *fire calendar*. A pure monthly-climatology predictor — firing on the zone-months with the highest historical share of that zone's annual fire, matched to the same 15.41% firing rate — scores **PSS 0.002** (precision 54.0%, indistinguishable from the base rate). The drought term's **PSS 0.116** is therefore almost entirely information the calendar does not contain. This is the central quantitative claim of the index and, to our knowledge, the first time it is measured.

**Leave-one-year-out validation of the drought term.** The natural objection to the figures above is that thresholds and events are defined on the same 13 years they are verified against. For the threshold side the objection dissolves into an algebraic property of the rule: a *top-2 of 13* threshold with a strict inequality is a rank condition — the year fires exactly when its value exceeds at least 11 of the *other* 12 years, since a value can never contribute to its own comparison. Re-deriving every zone-month threshold from the 12 remaining years (their 11th-smallest, the rank that preserves the operating point) and verifying each year against the threshold derived without it therefore reproduces the in-sample decisions identically by construction. We confirm this numerically — 7 of 18,096 firing decisions differ, all of them second-decimal rounding ties between the two implementations — giving LOYO precision **72.4%** and PSS **0.1163** against 72.3% and 0.1160 in-sample: **the trigger side of the skill in this section is out-of-sample by construction.** Two stricter variants probe what remains in-sample. Leaving the year out of the *event* definition as well (median of the 12 remaining same-calendar-months) gives precision **68.6%** and PSS **0.1206**: the LOYO median slightly raises the event rate, the precision pays for it, and the skill score holds. Re-deriving at the rank that preserves the *top-2* semantics on 12 years instead (their 10th-smallest, a looser operating point firing in 23.1% of zone-months) gives precision 70.5% and PSS **0.157** — the frozen threshold sits on the conservative side of its own skill curve, prioritizing precision over PSS as designed (bias 0.287), and the headroom at looser thresholds is noted rather than exploited.

**Leave-one-year-out validation of the ENSO term.** The ENSO factors are medians over 25 fire years, so the same protocol applies at the year level: for each fire year 2001–2025 the per-phase factors (lag 3, operational windows held fixed) are re-derived with that year excluded — the monthly climatological medians are recomputed without it as well, so the left-out year's anomalies are normalized by climatologies that never saw it — and the left-out year is read against them. The declared sign survives **all 25 folds in all four declared signals**: La Niña above El Niño in every fold for NOA, NEA and the country; El Niño above La Niña in every fold for Patagonia. The fold-to-fold factor ranges are narrow and never cross unity where the full-sample factor sits away from it (NOA La Niña 1.44–1.64, El Niño 0.87–0.97; Patagonia El Niño 0.98–1.32 against La Niña 0.82–0.91). Pooling the out-of-sample anomalies by phase reproduces the calibrated ordering, and for NOA it is nominally significant *out of sample* (median OOS anomaly 1.54 under La Niña against 0.88 under El Niño, La Niña-vs-rest *p* = 0.006); the other three signals hold their sign at *p* = 0.12–0.23 — the same *suggestive* support they carry in calibration, which leave-one-year-out neither strengthens nor weakens, and with ~6 substantial La Niña episodes in the record could not. The 8%-share windows themselves, re-derived without each VIIRS year, are unchanged in every fold for NOA, NEA and Patagonia (Centro changes in 1 of 14); the **country** window is the unstable one, changing in 7 of 14 folds as November oscillates around the 8% line — a fragility of the country aggregate taken up in §6.1b.

**Lead time.** The figures above are contemporaneous: DC of month *M* against fire of month *M*. Measured at increasing lead, precision decays toward the base rate:

| Lead | +0 | +1 | +2 | +3 | +4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Precision | 72.3% | 65.5% | 61.8% | 59.0% | 57.5% |
| Above base rate | +18.7 pp | +11.8 pp | +8.1 pp | +5.4 pp | +3.8 pp |

The term is strongest as a diagnosis of the month in progress and retains a usable edge out to roughly two months. The five-month anticipation of the 2020 Paraná Delta crisis — the southern-Delta zone of Entre Ríos exceeded its threshold from **March 2020**, and in August the zone opposite Rosario recorded **7,973** detections against a median of 78 — is a case study that motivated the zone resolution, and is reported as such: it carries no false-alarm accounting at that lead, and the aggregate table above is the honest measure of anticipation.

**Complementarity with fire weather.** The DC term is not a re-badged fire-weather index, though the two are nested by construction: the DC enters the FWI through the Build-Up Index, so the relevant claim is one of differing time constants, not of independence. At province-month resolution (3,744 cases, 576 activations each, percentile ranks of the provincial weighted means), the percentile ranks of monthly DC and monthly FWI correlate at *r* = 0.70, and the four cells are:

| | precision | *n* |
|---|---|---|
| both extreme | **92.4%** | 288 |
| DC only | **70.5%** | 288 |
| FWI only | **80.6%** | 288 |
| neither (base) | 48.8% | 2,880 |

Each index alone adds over the base, and their convergence adds most — but the monthly FWI alone is the stronger single discriminator of the two at this resolution, which is reported rather than omitted. The case for publishing both is that each adds over the other (DC over FWI-only: +11.8 pp; FWI over DC-only: +21.9 pp), not that the drought term dominates. Note also that this comparison is computed on the **provincial weighted mean**, the aggregation the escalation rule deliberately rejects (§3.2); a zone-resolution version of the comparison is pending.

**August 2020 case.** With the A-zonal rule, all **five** Litoral provinces of the 2020 crisis (Entre Ríos, Santa Fe, Corrientes, Chaco, Formosa) reach HIGH with the drought driver declared; under a provincial-mean rule Entre Ríos — the epicenter — stays below threshold (§3.2).

**Season gate.** Over 2012–2024, 178 unique (jurisdiction, calendar-month) pairs carry less than 8% of the jurisdiction's annual fire. The gate switches **145** of them to LOW; the **33** that survive do so because their forward window is still heavy (*r* ≥ 1.0), which the gate cannot override. Of the 178, nine have a near-zero observed record (median ≤ 5 detections/month over 13 years) and the gate reaches seven of those nine. The gate turns off **zero** months carrying ≥ 8% of annual fire — which, as noted in §3.3, is algebraically guaranteed rather than empirically discovered, and is stated here only to document that the implementation matches its specification. An earlier version of this note reported this result as "33 → 9"; that framing conflated the survivors with the near-zero set and is corrected here.

**Country-extent rule.** Under the coherent calibration — thresholds and operation both on 30-day trailing means (§3.2) — the extension condition activates in **18 of 156** hindcast months (≈ 1.4 per year), and **all 18** had country-level detections above the monthly median. The active set spans the country's major fire episodes — the 2013 winter–spring run (Aug–Oct), August 2020 and the extended 2020 drought (Mar–Dec), January 2022, and the 2022-12–2023-03 block — while quiet years (2014–2017, 2024) produce zero activations. Recomputed on calendar-month means as a sensitivity check, the same rule activates in 26 months at 84.6%: the operative statistic is now the better-performing one, which is the expected consequence of calibrating and operating on the same quantity. (Under the earlier, mismatched calibration the operative statistic gave 31 months at 83.9% against 19 at 100% for the calendar one — the discrepancy that motivated the re-derivation.)

## 5. Operational deployment

The index is live at geofuego.com.ar (public API: `ingesta.geofuego.com.ar/api/enso`), deployed on **2026-09-27**. A scheduled worker computes the daily DC for the 116 zones from Open-Meteo forecast-archive meteorology (with automatic backfill up to 92 days) into a persistent store keyed by zone and date; the classification engine evaluates the ENSO base, the A-zonal drought term, the season gate and the country-extent rule, and publishes per-jurisdiction levels with a one-line statement declaring the driver — ENSO phase, drought (naming the triggering zone), or both. Degradation is declared, never silent: if no DC data is available, the payload states that the level is ENSO-only. The calibration is versioned (2.2.0) and frozen in the deposit accompanying this note.

**Prospective registry.** Every level published since deployment is out-of-sample by construction and remains publicly inspectable in the product's hindcast view, which distinguishes the calibration period from the prospective record. Season-start predictions are frozen (versioned calibration, dated commit, Zenodo DOI); observed outcomes are scored at season end and published regardless of result; the calibration may be revised only **between** seasons. The 2026–27 season is the first out-of-sample test of the index.

## 6. Limitations

1. **Validation in progress.** All skill in §4 is measured over the calibration period with frozen rules. The leave-one-year-out analysis (§4) shows that the drought trigger is out-of-sample by construction, that its skill is positive in each of the 13 years, and that the declared ENSO signs survive every fold — but no resampling of 2012–2024 can supply data the calibration never saw in any form. That independent validation is the prospective record accumulating since 2026-09-27, season by season, in plain sight. The 2003–04 season — severe under neutral ENSO — is retained as the standing counterexample for the ENSO term: phase is a probabilistic risk factor, never a cause.

1b. **Window sensitivity.** An earlier version of this note applied a single July–November window to all four regions; it captured ~88% of NOA's fire but only ~25% of Patagonia's (peak December–February), so Patagonia's teleconnection was declared null without having been measured in its season. The current calibration derives each region's window from its own detection calendar (months with ≥ 8% of annual fire, §3.1), which is what surfaced the Patagonian inverse signal. The residual limitation is that the verdicts remain sensitive to the window rule itself: the country aggregate, for instance, weakens from nominal *p* = 0.03 under July–November to 0.22 under its data-derived July–October window, and no principled rule singles out 8% beyond its consistency with the season gate. The window rule is therefore declared as a fixed convention of the method, chosen once and not tuned per region. Measured, the sensitivity is asymmetric (windows re-derived at 6%, 8% and 10% share; `10_loyo_enso_ventana.mjs`): NOA, NEA and Centro are materially unchanged across the three thresholds — NOA's window and verdict are identical at 8% and 10%, and its *p* = 0.005 holds — while the country signal *strengthens* under the wider 6% window (lag-3 La Niña-vs-rest *p* = 0.018 nominal against 0.22 at 8%), so the 8% convention is the conservative choice for the aggregate. The one substantive fragility is **Patagonia's dependence on March**: at 10% share its window narrows to December–February and the lag-3 inverse separation disappears (El Niño 0.98, *p* = 0.73), while at 6% (which adds August–September) it persists (1.16, *p* = 0.052). The suggestive — not robust — verdict for Patagonia is in part *this* fragility made concrete, and the country window is also the least stable under leave-one-year-out (§4).

1c. **Multiplicity.** The factors are computed over five domains, five lags and two permutation contrasts (Appendix A): 50 tests. No correction is applied to the reported *p*-values, and at the Bonferroni threshold of 0.001 **no cell survives** — the best is NOA at lags 0 and 3 (*p* = 0.005). The robust declaration for NOA therefore rests on the stability of its sign and effect size across lags 0–6, not on surviving a correction; the suggestive declarations rest on sign stability alone. The country aggregate is not independent of NOA and NEA. The permutation test also compares means of log-anomalies while the published factor is a median, so the *p*-value does not test the reported statistic.
2. **The index adds signals.** Accumulated drought raises the level; wet ground leaves the level at its base, and the statement qualifies it (the phase is declared as the only driver). A dedicated wet-ground reduction was evaluated and measured against the archive: 28.3% of its reductions coincided with above-median fire months, so declaring the base level was chosen over reducing it.
3. **Hydrological amplifiers.** Episodes like the Paraná low-water stand — which leaves wetland fuel exposed and continuous — belong to the index's next layer; integrating the corridor's hydrometry is the declared line of work.
4. **Resolution in wetlands.** The DC models the forest soil of the Canadian system and ERA5 averages ~25 km cells; in wetlands the value works as a regional drought tendency — a useful approximation, read alongside the satellite detection record that captures what the model does not represent.
5. **Threshold resolution, and what "p90" means here.** With a 13-year climatology the threshold is exceeded only by the top one or two values of the record: the measured exceedance rate is 15.4% of zone-months, not 10%. The label is retained for continuity with the calibration file, but the operative rule is *top-2 of 13* and should be read as such. The threshold's resolution grows with every added year of the series.

5b. **Calibrated versus operated statistic — resolved.** An earlier calibration derived the thresholds from **calendar-month mean** DC while production compared a **30-day trailing mean** against them; the two are close but not interchangeable (under that mismatch the country-extent rule gave 31 active months at 83.9% on the operated statistic against 19 at 100% on the calibrated one). As of calibration 2.2.0 the thresholds are derived from the operated statistic itself — the 13 end-of-month trailing means (§3.2) — and every hindcast figure in §4 is computed with it; the headline skill barely moved (precision 72.4% → 72.3%, PSS 0.117 → 0.116), which shows the skill was never an artefact of the mismatch, while the country-extent rule tightened to 18 months at 100% (§4). The residual approximation is the monthly granularity of the threshold: a trailing window closing early in month *M* samples mostly *M*−1 but is compared against *M*'s end-of-month climatology; the hindcast, which evaluates at month ends, does not measure that intra-month effect.

5c. **Detection is not independent of drought.** The verification target is FIRMS detection counts, and drought reduces cloud cover, which raises detection probability regardless of actual fire. Part of the drought term's measured skill may therefore reflect improved observability rather than increased burning. We have not attempted to separate the two; doing so requires a cloud-fraction covariate in the verification and is declared work. This cuts in the same direction as limitation 7 but is a bias on the skill figures, not only on the detections.
6. **Sample and granularity of the ENSO term.** 2001–2025 contains ~6 substantial La Niña episodes, but at lag 3 within NOA's August–November window only **12 month-observations** fall in the La Niña phase — roughly three seasons of four months, not six episodes, because few La Niñas are already installed by autumn. Intra-episode autocorrelation makes the nominal permutation *p*-values optimistic on top of that, and each province inherits its regional factor (mapping declared). The drought term, by contrast, is measured per zone. The ENSO term should be read as the weaker of the two components, and it is the one that only sets a base level.
7. **Detection ≠ fire.** FIRMS thermal anomalies include residual false positives and miss fires under dense cloud; low-confidence detections and static sources are filtered as declared in the product methodology.
8. **Alerting authority rests with the SNMF and civil-defense agencies**; the GFI informs prevention and planning.

## 7. Code and data availability

Active-fire data: NASA FIRMS (public). ONI: NOAA/CPC (public). Meteorology: ERA5 via the Open-Meteo archive API (public). The full method package — this note, the calibration file (`enso_calibracion.json`, version 2.2.0, including the 116 zones and their monthly DC thresholds), the fire-zone definitions (`zonas_fuego.json`), the classification engine (`enso_logic.js`, byte-identical to the logic run in production), the ENSO calibration derivation script (`derive_enso_calibracion.mjs`), the fire-zone DC-threshold derivation script (`derive_calibracion_v2.mjs`), the point-in-polygon province boundaries (`provincias_pip.js`), the hindcast generator (`gen_gfi_retro.mjs`) and its output (`gfi_v2_retro.json`), and the verification scripts (`05a_focos_por_zona.mjs`, `05b_valida_zonal_fwi.py`, `06_verificacion.py`, `07_barrido_lags.mjs`, `09_loyo_dc.py`, `10_loyo_enso_ventana.mjs`) — is published as an open, citable deposit (https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23017740; see License and citation). `05b` reproduces the zone-level skill and the DC × FWI cells; `06_verificacion.py` reproduces the contingency table, the skill scores with their year-block confidence intervals, the climatology baseline, the lead-time table, the season-gate counts and the country-extent rule under both statistics; `07_barrido_lags.mjs` reproduces Appendix A; `08_ventana_por_region.py` reproduces the seasonal distribution of fire by region that underlies limitation 1b; `09_loyo_dc.py` reproduces the leave-one-year-out validation of the drought term (pooled variants and per-year table) and `10_loyo_enso_ventana.mjs` the leave-one-year-out of the ENSO term and the 6/8/10% window-sensitivity sweep of limitation 1b. Together they cover every figure in Section 4, Appendix A and the limitations that carry numbers. The scripts require the raw FIRMS, ONI and ERA5 series, which are not redistributed — `DATOS.md` in the deposit documents how to fetch each one from its public source, in what order to run the scripts, and which figures each one produces. The operational product infrastructure remains proprietary; the live per-jurisdiction state is publicly served by the API above.
